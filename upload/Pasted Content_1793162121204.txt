# PROJECT: PMIST VOICE AI ADMISSION ASSISTANT
## Complete Development Specification for an Offline, Multilingual AI Application

### 1. YOUR ROLE

Act as a senior full-stack developer, local AI engineer, speech-processing engineer, and UI/UX designer.

Build a complete, functional, offline AI-powered voice assistant for the admission department of **Periyar Maniammai Institute of Science and Technology (PMIST), Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, India**.

The application should interact naturally with prospective students, understand their interests and favourite subjects, recommend suitable courses from the supplied PMIST course document, and generate personalized educational and career roadmaps.

This is a real software development project intended for a college submission with a very short deadline.

**Do not deliver only a UI mockup, a static website, or a basic text-only chatbot. Build an integrated application with a working backend, local AI, voice input, voice output, verified course recommendations, and personalized roadmaps.**

Prioritize a reliable, demonstrable end-to-end prototype over unnecessary features.

The developer has supplied the following reference document:

`PMIST_Open_House_Course_Career_Chatbot.docx`

## 2.1 Course information restrictions

The assistant must follow these rules in every conversation.

**Rule 1: Recommend only document-listed courses.**

Every recommended course must match a course record extracted from the supplied document and stored in the application's local course database.

**Rule 2: Never invent a PMIST course.**

The AI must not create new course names, specializations, combinations, degree programmes, or alternative course titles.

For example, if a student expresses interest in game development, the assistant may consider a relevant listed course such as B.Tech. CSE with specialization in AR & VR, if supported by the document. However, it must never claim that PMIST offers a dedicated game development degree unless that exact course is included in the approved database.

**Rule 3: Preserve official course names.**

Use the exact course names recorded in the source document.

You may explain a course in simpler language, but you must not modify its official name.

**Rule 4: Use only supplied course details.**

Course descriptions, associated interests, skills, learning roadmaps, and career directions must be grounded in the supplied document.

You may personalize the order, wording, and emphasis of those details according to a student's interests.

You must not introduce unsupported institutional facts.

**Rule 5: Do not invent eligibility information.**

Never tell a student that they qualify for a course unless verified eligibility requirements and the student's relevant academic information are available in an explicitly approved source.

The supplied course document alone must not be treated as proof of eligibility.

**Rule 6: Do not invent official curriculum details.**

If the document does not provide a verified semester-wise syllabus, do not generate one.

You may provide a general skills roadmap based on the supplied document, but label it clearly as suggested guidance rather than the official PMIST curriculum.

**Rule 7: Do not invent admission or placement facts.**

Do not fabricate:

- Admission cutoffs.
- Fees or scholarships.
- Placement percentages.
- Average or highest salary packages.
- Admission deadlines.
- Accreditation status.
- Course duration.
- Eligibility criteria.
- Seat availability.
- Semester-wise subjects.
- Current admission status.
- Institutional facilities.
- Internship guarantees.

If information is missing, explain that the assistant does not have verified information about it and recommend contacting the PMIST admission department.

**Rule 8: Never silently substitute external information.**

Do not use internet searches, external APIs, or the language model's internal knowledge to supplement the course database.

Do not automatically download a newer course list.

Do not silently update institutional information.

Any future source changes must be made explicitly by the developer.

## 2.2 Distinguish listed courses from currently verified offerings

The supplied document identifies itself as a working inventory and warns that availability, nomenclature, specializations, duration, eligibility, and admission year may change.

Therefore, the application must not claim that every listed course is currently open for admission.

Implement a course-status field, such as:

- `listed_unverified`: Present in the supplied document but not independently confirmed as currently available.
- `verified_current`: Explicitly verified by the developer against an approved current official source.
- `inactive`: Explicitly marked inactive by the developer.

Initially, imported courses must not automatically become `verified_current`.

For the first prototype, the assistant may recommend relevant courses from the supplied inventory while clearly stating that current availability must be confirmed with the admission department.

The application must not claim that an unverified course is currently accepting applications.

If no current verification has been supplied, explain the limitation rather than inventing confirmation.

# 3. PROJECT OBJECTIVE

Build a friendly, multilingual admission assistant that completes the following workflow:

1. Greet the student.
2. Ask for the student's name.
3. Identify the language used by the student.
4. Build a natural conversation.
5. Ask about favourite subjects and interests.
6. Ask relevant follow-up questions only when needed.
7. Identify the student's academic interests and possible career goals.
8. Retrieve suitable courses from the local PMIST course database.
9. Match student interests against the retrieved courses using backend logic.
10. Validate all recommended courses against the approved database.
11. Explain why each course may suit the student's interests.
12. Generate a personalized educational and career roadmap.
13. Display recommendations and the roadmap in the React interface.
14. Read the assistant's responses aloud using local text-to-speech.
15. Allow the student to continue asking questions through voice or text.
16. Work offline after the required software, dependencies, and models have been installed.

The assistant should feel like a helpful admission counsellor, not a form that mechanically asks a fixed set of questions.

# 7. CONVERSATION DESIGN

Implement a conversation manager that maintains the student's temporary session context.

The assistant must ask relevant questions naturally and avoid repeating information already provided.

## Stage 1: Welcome

Example:

Assistant:

"Hi! Welcome to PMIST! 😊 What's your name?"

Student:

"My name is Arjun."

Assistant:

"Nice to meet you, Arjun! How are you today?"

The assistant should be warm, respectful, professional, and suitable for prospective college students.

Do not use excessive emojis or overly casual language.

## Stage 2: Discover interests

Ask short questions, such as:

- Which subjects do you enjoy studying?
- What is your favourite subject?
- Do you enjoy solving mathematical problems?
- Are you interested in programming, designing, business, science, or engineering?
- What activities do you enjoy?
- What would you like to do in your future career?

Do not ask all these questions mechanically.

Ask follow-up questions based on what the student has already said.

For example:

Student:

"I like computer science."

Assistant:

"That's great! Do you enjoy coding, analysing data, creating digital experiences, or understanding how computer systems work?"

This is an illustrative interaction, not a statement about course availability.

## Stage 3: Understand the student's intent

Use the local AI model to extract structured information from the conversation.

Maintain fields such as:

{
  "student_name": null,
  "preferred_language": "English",
  "favourite_subjects": [],
  "interests": [],
  "preferred_activities": [],
  "career_interests": [],
  "academic_background": null,
  "clarification_needed": false,
  "conversation_stage": "discover_interests"
}

Use `null` or empty arrays when information is unknown.

Do not fabricate missing details.

Do not assume that liking a subject means the student qualifies for a particular programme.

Do not repeatedly ask for the student's name, favourite subject, or career interest after the student has already provided that information.

If the student's interests are unclear, ask one short clarification question.

## Stage 4: Recommend courses

Once sufficient information has been collected:

1. Extract the student's interests.
2. Send the structured interests to the backend course matcher.
3. Retrieve candidate courses from `courses.json`.
4. Score candidates using explicit backend logic.
5. Exclude courses that do not match an approved database record.
6. Apply availability-status rules.
7. Return a small number of suitable recommendations.
8. Generate an explanation using the retrieved course records.
9. Validate the final response before displaying it.

The assistant should normally present two or three relevant options when sufficient matches exist.

If only one suitable course is identified, present one.

Do not manufacture additional recommendations to reach a target number.

If no suitable match is found, explain that the assistant could not identify a suitable course from the available records.

Offer to show the supplied course inventory or suggest contacting the admission department.

## Stage 5: Explain the recommendations

For each course, explain:

- Official course name.
- Degree level, where supported by the source.
- Relevant student interests.
- Why the course may suit those interests.
- Relevant foundational skills.
- Suggested learning roadmap.
- Possible career directions from the supplied document.
- Any important uncertainty about current availability.

Keep explanations understandable to a student who has little knowledge of college programmes.

Use simple English or the student's preferred language.

Do not claim that a course guarantees a particular career or salary.

Do not make definitive statements about which course is best for the student.

Use language such as:

"Based on the interests you shared, this course may be worth exploring."

"This is a suggestion, not a final decision about your future."

"Please confirm the current availability and admission requirements with PMIST."

# 8. BACKEND COURSE RECOMMENDATION ENGINE

This component is the most important protection against fabricated course information.

Do not depend on the language model's system prompt alone.

Implement a deterministic course matcher.

Suggested file:

`server/services/courseMatcher.js`

## 8.1 Interest extraction

Use the local language model to extract relevant information from the student's conversation.

For example:

Student input:

"I like maths, programming and building small applications."

Possible extracted interests:

- Mathematics.
- Programming.
- Software development.
- Problem solving.

The extraction model may identify synonyms, but it must not generate course names.

Validate the extracted data structure before using it.

If the model returns malformed JSON, retry safely or use a basic rule-based fallback.

## 8.2 Deterministic matching

Use a combination of:

- Keyword matching.
- Synonym mapping.
- Subject-to-course mappings from the supplied document.
- Interest tags derived from the document.
- A simple transparent relevance score.

Example:

A student expresses interest in biology, computing, and analysing biological data.

The backend may identify a relevant course if the supplied document associates those interests with a listed programme.

Do not use unrelated external course knowledge to improve the match.

## 8.3 Ranking

Assign relevance scores using a transparent algorithm.

For example:

- Direct match with a favourite subject: 3 points.
- Match with a stated personal interest: 3 points.
- Match with a preferred activity: 2 points.
- Match with a stated career interest: 2 points.

These weights are implementation choices, not institutional facts.

Normalize or cap scores as appropriate.

Return the most relevant database-listed records.

Do not allow the language model to assign arbitrary scores to courses that the backend has not retrieved.

## 8.4 Strict course validation

Before a recommendation reaches the frontend:

1. Confirm that the course ID exists in `courses.json`.
2. Confirm that the official course name matches the stored record.
3. Confirm that all displayed course-specific details originate from that record.
4. Confirm that the course is not marked inactive.
5. Check its availability status.
6. Remove any unapproved course from the response.
7. Reject the response if validation fails.

Use stable course IDs internally.

Never trust course names returned by the language model without validating them against the database.

The frontend must display courses returned by the backend, not courses independently invented by the AI.

**Critical acceptance test:** If the language model generates a nonexistent course name, the backend must prevent that name from appearing as a PMIST recommendation.

## 8.5 No-match behavior

If no relevant course is found, return a structured response such as:

{
  "status": "no_match",
  "message": "I could not identify a suitable course from the available PMIST course records.",
  "recommendations": [],
  "next_action": "show_available_courses_or_contact_admissions"
}

The exact message can be localized.

Never invent a new course to avoid an empty result.

# 13. COMPLETE VOICE INTERACTION LOOP

Implement the complete following process:

1. Student presses the microphone button.
2. The browser requests microphone permission if necessary.
3. The interface displays a recording indicator.
4. Audio is captured.
5. Recording stops when the student finishes or presses stop.
6. The local speech recognition engine converts audio into text.
7. Recognized text appears in the conversation.
8. The frontend sends the text to the Express backend.
9. The backend updates the conversation state.
10. The local AI identifies the student's intent.
11. The backend retrieves course records when needed.
12. The course matcher identifies relevant database-listed courses.
13. The backend validates the recommendations.
14. The local AI generates a conversational explanation using the approved data.
15. The backend validates the final response.
16. The response appears in the chat interface.
17. The local TTS engine generates audio.
18. The frontend plays the audio.
19. Playback stops before the next microphone recording begins.
20. The student can ask another question using voice or text.

Do not skip the actual speech recognition or speech synthesis stages.

Do not simulate a microphone interaction by displaying prerecorded text.

Do not use the browser's cloud-dependent speech recognition implementation as a substitute for the required local engine.

If the selected implementation cannot complete a stage, clearly document that limitation and provide the nearest working local implementation.
